## Approve an app's sign-in without Cloudflare Access

- **Signed in to the app, you can approve an app's sign-in.** When claude.ai, ChatGPT, Cursor or another app connects with OAuth (`OAUTH_SIGNING_KEY` set), its approval page at `/oauth/authorize` now takes the session you signed in to `/app` with, by owner key. Before, only Cloudflare Access could approve, so on the compose deploy without Access nobody could, and every app's sign-in stopped at "Sign in first". Behind Access, Access still works as before, and the session works there too. The page says the app will act "as you (the owner)".
- **Agent claims still need Access.** The 6-digit claim at `/oauth/claim` (an agent following `/auth.md`) is not approved by the session: it still needs Cloudflare Access in front.
- **Coming from the app, one click more.** Your session cookie is `SameSite=Strict`, so a browser sent to Yardhand by claude.ai or ChatGPT leaves it out. Yardhand then shows a page, "Continue to approve this app in Yardhand", whose one link opens the same approval with the cookie. Nothing about the cookie changed.
- **Not signed in, the page says where to.** The approval page links to `/app/sign-in`, and once you sign in the app takes you straight back to the approval. The app goes back only to `/oauth/authorize` on its own address, never anywhere else.
- **For scripts.** An approval must be posted from Yardhand's own page: a POST to `/oauth/authorize` with the session needs `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`, or an `Origin` of this address when the browser sends no `Sec-Fetch-Site`. An `x-gateway-session` header sent to the gateway is now dropped: only the gateway sets it, and before, one sent in made `GET /api/session` answer `signedIn: true`.

Nothing to do by hand: no setting and no data change.

## A log of what each app sends as it signs in

- **One line per step, on the gateway's log.** While `OAUTH_SIGNING_KEY` is set, every `POST /oauth/register`, `GET /oauth/authorize` and `POST /oauth/token` writes a line starting `sign-in` (read it with `docker compose logs gateway | grep sign-in`, or `kubectl logs` with Helm). It says which app (the name it registered, and whether it registered here or came by a client_id URL), the `resource` it named and its value, where it returns to (scheme and host only), whether it sent `redirect_uri` with the token request, `Sec-Fetch-Site` and the `Referer`'s origin, and the address that registered it. A short id ties one app's lines together.
- **Never a secret.** No code, verifier, token, `state`, approval ticket, cookie or client_id is written, a `resource` with a query, or a user and password, is cut to its origin and path, and nothing is kept in the database: the lines live only as long as your logs do.
- **Why.** The next releases bind each token to the address it was asked for, and the rules for that are settled on what real apps send. If you connect apps to Yardhand while on this version, these lines are what tells. It goes again in 0.7.0.

Nothing to do by hand.
